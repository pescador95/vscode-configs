# Business Documentation Rules

## Purpose

Business documentation describes what the system does and why it exists from the business perspective.

Document applicable:

* domains;
* actors and responsibilities;
* business entities;
* business rules;
* processes and workflows;
* states and transitions;
* functional requirements;
* non-functional requirements;
* constraints;
* validations;
* integrations relevant to business behavior.

## Business Perspective

Focus on business intent, behavior, rules, decisions, and outcomes.

Avoid unnecessary implementation details such as classes, packages, frameworks, database internals, or infrastructure unless they are relevant to understanding the business behavior.

## Evidence

Business documentation must be supported by requirements, domain behavior, existing documentation, or other reliable project evidence.

Never invent business rules or requirements.

## Terminology

Preserve established business terminology and domain definitions.

When terminology is ambiguous or conflicting, identify the ambiguity instead of silently redefining the concept.
